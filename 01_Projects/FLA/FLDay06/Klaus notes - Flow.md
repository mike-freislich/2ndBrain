## 2. Lage der Nation - Klaus

> I want to start with a few words about why I think Flight Levels is more relevant right now than ever.
>
> We’re living in difficult times. Multiple crises on the planet. Companies under financial pressure. A high degree of uncertainty. And some mornings you wake up, check the news, and think: “Okay, interesting. Seems like someone made a few new creative decisions while I was asleep.”
>
> And on top of all that, we have Artificial Intelligence.
>
> Depending on whom you listen to, AI will either save everything, destroy everything, or at least produce 47 PowerPoint decks and 18 LinkedIn Posts before lunch.
>
> I’m really excited about AI. But I also think many organizations are looking at it too narrowly.
>
> If you just put AI into existing workflows, existing silos, existing decision structures, and existing bottlenecks, you don’t automatically get a better organization.
>
> Very often, AI does not increase the performance of the system. It only increases the pressure in the system.
>
> More ideas. More analysis. More code. More content. More everything. But if prioritization is still slow, decisions are still unclear, dependencies are still invisible, and teams are still locally optimized, then the bottleneck just moves somewhere else.
>
> That’s why Flight Levels matters more than ever before!
>
> Flight Levels is about understanding how work flows through an organization. Where strategy meets execution. Where teams depend on each other. Where decisions get stuck. Where local optimization creates system-wide pain.
>
> And now, with AI becoming part of the operating model, this system view becomes even more important. Because AI is not just another tool on the side. It becomes part of how work moves.
>
> And let’s be honest: if work becomes more complex, sticky notes and meeting discipline are not enough. You also need a tool that can handle the complexity of modern work — dependencies, flow, coordination across teams, and strategy connected to execution.
>
> That is one reason why we’re so happy to run this Flight Levels Day together with Businessmap. Because Businessmap is built for exactly that kind of complexity. It gives Flight Levels systems a place to live.
>
> So today is not a method show. It’s not framework karaoke. It’s about real stories from real organizations putting Flight Levels to work.

## 3. How the day works - Mike

> Thanks, Klaus.
>
> Let me quickly explain how today works.
>
> We have two stages: **Stage One** and **Stage Two**.
>
> Stage One is mostly the more technical and Businessmap-oriented track. You’ll see concrete Flight Level 2 systems, flow, dependencies, coordination, and tool-supported work.
>
> Stage Two is more about Flight Levels in use without a specific tool focus: small companies, strategy execution, AI, leadership, mindset, and measurement.
>
> Below the stage — so under the video you’re watching right now — you can see the full program.
>
> You can click on any program item to switch to that talk. Just one important detail: if you click on a talk that starts at 15:00 while it’s still 12:10, the stage will be empty. That’s not a bug. That’s time behaving normally.
>
> So follow the schedule, choose the talk you want to watch, and switch between Stage One and Stage Two whenever you need to.

## 4. Stages and Zoom Q&A - Mike

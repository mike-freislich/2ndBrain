 1. **⁠What ethical issue do you think is most visible or important in our community or daily life right now?**

The "rights" and "access" that public and private enterprises seem to have regarding private individuals personal information, and their use of said information, to exploit the market.

 2. I am researching ethics in the following area: Influencer Culture & Advertising. How have you personally experienced or been affected by this issue?

I feel too old to care too much about the direct contribution of influencers. I don't have tiktok or instagram, only use facebook for business, and typically steer clear of obvious influencer content on youtube. That being said, in the world of advertising and obviously fake or uninformed content is now prolific, where ever I look on the internet. News is sensationalized, and it feels like 80% of "headlines" are simply clickbait, geared at ensnaring an unsuspecting user. There's too much noise. Too many so-called experts. Real information has been drowned out by all the internet slop. Even call centre direct-marketing has become close to the levels I experienced in the USA some years back. Almost every call I receive on my mobile, is someone trying to sell me insurance. Scammers are on the rise and getting more intelligent.

3. How did you deal with / manage the issue personally?

I try not to take the bait. I have implemented aggressive anti-advertisement technology on my home network. I pay for you tube, to remove adverts. I don't have any television subscriptions, only on-demand video. I have uninstalled most social media apps. I have a list of trusted websites where I get mostly real information. I have truecaller on my phone to screen most spam/scam calls. I spam-filter my email, and auto unsubscribe from any unsolicited emails. More and more, I use A.I. instead of "search" for my research.

4. From your own understanding, how do you understand ethical versus unethical behaviour in this area?

Unethical behaviour often borders on criminal. Some examples:
- Marketing that pulls you in with claims about massive savings or super low pricing, only to charge more than advertised on checkout
- Direct Marketing calls that bypass the national-opt-out database
- Falsifying facts, or making up "true" stories and masking them as "real life"
- Scams and Phishing
- Large corporates that sell my personal information, so that other companies can target me.
- Posting videos of somebody else without their explicit permission.

Ethical
- Sell me a cheap product that I want, and offer me related products on checkout - my choice.
- Start a call by stating, "this is a direct marketing call. I got your number from x.  would you like to continue, or shall we remove you from our database?"
- Present known, checkable facts, or prefix your story with, "this is made up" or "this is my opinion".
- Large corporates that clearly ask your permission to pass your details on to an explicit network of companies, that are likely to add value - my choice.

I think there are many people who understand the ethics, even if just by seeing the behaviour as "annoying". Usually the common thread behind unethical behaviour is self-gain.
